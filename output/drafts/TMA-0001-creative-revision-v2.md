# TMA-0001 creative revision v2

## Brief

Preserve the central premise: a man discovers tomorrow's newspaper contains
events that have not happened yet. Make the story clearer, more suspenseful and
more memorable in 45–60 seconds. Do not add characters or subplots. Do not
render yet.

## What changed from v1

| Problem in v1 | Change in v2 |
|---|---|
| Opening corridor is static; the mystery arrives after the first shot | Opens on the obituary itself: tomorrow's date, Elias's name, 3:07 a.m. The page fills the frame before we see him alive |
| Several scenes repeat the paper-grid and corridor compositions | Ten distinct compositions: tabletop macro, corridor wide, doorway two-shot, heater close-up, stairwell wide, window frame-within-frame, desk high-angle, obituary macro, overhead mandala, face close-up |
| Causal chain is loose: the fire simply happens; the neighbour appears late | The neighbour's faulty heater is the fire's cause; she asked Elias to fix it; he leaves; she enters 4B to warn him and dies |
| The newspaper's rule is vague ("it was deciding what to keep") | The fine print states "one name per fire"; the second edition substitutes Mara Kwan; every saved edition has taken a name |
| Tw ist is intriguing but under-set-up | The final line explains the rule without explaining away the dread: "It was choosing who paid for it." |

## Ending options

### A. The Substitution — selected

Elias avoids the fire. The next edition names Mara Kwan, the neighbour who went
in to warn him. He realizes the paper does not forecast death; it assigns one
name per fire, and his escape moved the cost to her.

**Why it wins:** It is the only ending that pays off the neighbour, the heater
and the fine print in one move. It is surprising on first watch and completely
legible on second watch. The final line is short, declarative and reframes the
opening obituary.

### B. The Loop — rejected

Elias burns the paper and stays. At 3:07 nothing happens. In the morning a new
paper arrives with Mara's name, pushed under the door by a hand he recognizes as
his own.

**Why it loses:** It is atmospheric but adds a time-loop rule the story has not
set up. It also makes the neighbour's death incidental rather than caused by
Elias's choice.

### C. The Sacrifice — rejected

Elias returns to the burning building and dies pulling Mara out. The next paper
prints both names. Final line: "The Archive always gets its story."

**Why it loses:** It is morally tidy and visually strong, but it removes the
central unease. The paper becomes a generic collector rather than a system that
trades one life for another. It also makes the final line a slogan instead of a
realization.

## Selected structure

1. **0.0–7.0 s — The obituary.** Macro on tomorrow's paper: Elias Voss, 4B,
   fire, 3:07 a.m. Living hands hold the page; he is alive.
2. **7.0–12.2 s — The rule.** Wide corridor. No subscription. Fine print:
   one name per fire.
3. **12.2–19.0 s — The cause.** He knocks on 4A. Mara's coil heater is against
   a curtain; she asked him to fix it.
4. **19.0–21.6 s — The dismissal.** Macro on the dial at maximum. "The super
   would come."
5. **21.6–25.4 s — The exit.** Low-angle stairwell. He does not look back.
6. **25.4–30.2 s — The fire.** Hotel window; smoke across the rooftops;
   4B burns at 3:07.
7. **30.2–33.2 s — The next edition.** High-angle hotel desk; a fresh paper
   waits.
8. **33.2–38.4 s — The substitution.** Macro on Mara Kwan, 4A, smoke.
9. **38.4–47.0 s — The ledger.** Overhead radial spread of editions; each has
   taken a name; she went in to warn him.
10. **47.0–52.2 s — The reframe.** Close-up; final line. Subtle wordmark only;
    no logo intro or end card.

## Opening and brand

The first frame is the future obituary, not a logo. The existing low-opacity
wordmark remains in the top margin of the generated stills; it never occupies a
separate card and never delays the story.
