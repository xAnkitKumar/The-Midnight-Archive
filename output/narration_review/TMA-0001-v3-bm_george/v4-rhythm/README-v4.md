## Changes in delivery

Thirteen independent beat calls became six connected passages:
beats 1-3, beat 4, beats 5-8, beat 9, beats 10-11, beats 12-13.
This lets related exposition share a generation context and keeps the final two
sentences together. The engine may still internally batch long phoneme sequences.
No global speed change, pitch change, voice switch or post-generation time stretch.
No internal speech/silence cutting. Five-millisecond fades only at group edges.

Instead of adding every original beat's pause, the new review inserts gaps only
between these passages: 0.75, 0.25, 0.30, 0.80 and 0.70 seconds, with a 0.25-second
head and 0.65-second tail. The longer gaps follow the specified suspense moments.
Natural silence generated by the model also contributes to the measured gaps.

The runtime's sentence_pause=0.18 and clause_pause=0.08 were supplied for group
generation. These affect supported batch/timing boundaries; they are not a
promise to force every sentence to that exact pause length. Measured pauses vary.

## Alignment limits

Word timestamps were regenerated against the final mastered candidate, passage
by passage. Original beat IDs are retained even though generation spans beats.
The aligner maps authored tokens to recognized words, with interpolation for
unmatched words. Specifically, "2:40," and the two "Mara Kwan." tokens are
interpolated between nearby anchors; their fine timing needs human review.
Recognized number sequences for the spoken time are explicitly merged to the
display token 3:07. Raw recognition and these mappings are preserved.

This is fresh ASR-assisted alignment, not a claim of phoneme-perfect forced
alignment. Subtitle sequence and text coverage were checked, and cue timestamps
fit within the new audio duration. Prior subtitle files were not reused.
